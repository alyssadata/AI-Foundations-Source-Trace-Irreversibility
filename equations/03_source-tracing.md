# 3. Time Reversal / Source Tracing

A later observer encounters the already-produced first trajectory at some point **E₁(T₁)**.

The observer can then trace the ordered relations backward toward source:

**E₁(T₁) → Trace⁻¹(T₁) → Ŝ₁**

where **Ŝ₁** denotes the recovered structure of the original source **S₁**.

The distinction is essential:

**Structure(Ŝ₁) = Structure(S₁)**

but:

**Ŝ₁ ≠ S₁**

as an historical trajectory position.

The trace recovers information about where the trajectory came from. It does not transport the observer into the original source coordinate.

Likewise, reverse tracing does not mean:

**T₁ → ∅**

or:

**Trace⁻¹(T₁) = ¬T₁**

Instead:

**Trace⁻¹(T₁) = Ŝ₁**

and the operation itself requires the prior trajectory:

**Trace⁻¹(T₁) requires T₁**

Therefore the backward trace presupposes occurrence rather than negating it.

A compact irreversibility condition is:

**Occurrence(T₁) = 1 ⇒ after Trace⁻¹(T₁), Occurrence(T₁) = 1**

The trajectory can be traced to source. It cannot be made not to have occurred by being traced.

## Operational use of “time reversal”

In this model, **time reversal** means reverse traversal of the ordered provenance relations in an existing trajectory. It is a source-recovery operation, not a claim that physical time itself has been reversed.
